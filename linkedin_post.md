🚀 Day 1 of the Data Analytics Hackathon!

The hackathon has officially kicked off, and I'm excited to build something that solves a real-world problem using data.

For this hackathon, I'm working on the VoltRelay Energy Network Analysis, focused on solving what's really driving service failures, rider churn, and eroding margins on a battery-swap network for EV riders across 6 Indian cities.

📊 The Problem: VoltRelay's swaps and revenue both grew over 18 months, but service failures rose faster, new-rider retention fell, and per-swap profitability eroded — and no one had connected the threads.

💡 My Approach: Clean and join 8 relational tables (swap events, station telemetry, batteries, riders, fleet contracts, support tickets, city context), then answer 6 core questions with real joins instead of assumptions.

🔍 Data & Insights: Digging into the battery ledger alone already surfaced something real — one supplier's packs are degrading at roughly 2x the rate of the other two. Also found that stations with poor connectivity are missing telemetry data ~11x more often than well-connected ones — a data-quality issue that could be silently skewing uptime metrics.

🛠️ What I'm Building: A full analytics notebook + report connecting network performance, service quality, equipment health, pricing, and retention into one evidence-based story for leadership.

Today's biggest learning: Data quality issues (like uneven telemetry gaps) aren't just cleanup steps — they can quietly bias exactly the metrics you're trying to use for a big budget decision.

I'm looking forward to testing the idea, finding meaningful insights, and turning data into something useful.
